# Changelog

## 2026-10-21
Title: Animation Out of the Box, Smooth Motion, Planted Feet and Grabs That Hold
### Added
# Board
Board model levels of detail - Skateboard.BoardModelLevels takes a board model's lower levels of detail as models of their own (a model's own lower levels cannot be read from code). Each is taken apart along the parts found on BoardModel, each triangle going to the part of the first level's nearest one, so its wheels spin and trucks steer as the first level's do, and each part switches where BoardModel's LOD groups do. The split board drew its first level at every distance
Boards with something under the deck - BoardPartDetector measures a deck with something hung under its middle (a case, a tank) beside it, along its centre line toward the axles: it took the hanging thing's underside for the deck's and turned the board away as having nothing under its deck. Boards measured as before keep every measurement
# Controls
Shaking before it flies - SkateRider.RecallShake (seconds, 0 by default) has a board called from more than 200 in away tremble where it lies first, growing stronger and rising up to 5 in off the ground (jittering up to 1.5 in and 14 degrees), then fly from there
Board called - SkateRider.BoardCalled( shake, flight ) is raised where the rider is simulated as its board is called to the hand, with the seconds it shakes and then flies, so a game can time a sound or an effect to it
Recall range and sight - SkateRider.RecallRange (metres, 30 by default) sets how far the board is called back to the hand from, and SkateRider.RecallNeedsSight (on by default) whether it must be in sight; off, it flies back through whatever is in the way. From farther than 30 m it comes at 70 m/s (BoardReach.RecallSeconds; within 30 m it flies as before)
Stand at - SkateRider.StandAt( place ) puts a riding rider on its feet at a place of the game's choosing (a player taking its place there), leaving the board where it is; unlike Dismount it needs no room beside the board
Responsive walking - SkateRider.ResponsiveWalking makes walking and carrying the board answer the controls as a game's own player controller does: full speed in about 0.15 s (was 0.36), a stop in about 0.2 s, a turn keeps its speed while the way the rider goes swings round at up to 1080 degrees a second and the body follows at 900, and pushing the stick takes over from a landing's clip. A turn no longer slows the rider into a stop clip and runs on out of it, and letting go for under a tenth of a second (going from one key to another) keeps the walk's clips going. Off (the default) walking eases in and out as before
# Networking
Other players walk and carry - another player's rider walks, carries the board and rides as its owner does: the owner's mode is shared, and its walk and run are shown from how fast its synced place moves
Boards simulated by their owner - a board is now simulated by its network owner, or by the host when it has none, instead of always by the host. Give the rider ownership (GameObject.Network.AssignOwnership) and the rider's own client simulates the board it rides: its input reaches the board with no round trip, and the host pays for no board but those nobody owns. A peer taking a board over carries on from where the board was last seen; input set where a board is not simulated goes to its owner (from the driver only, which the host still chooses). The board's synced state (speed, wheels, air, bail, balance, grind) is written by whoever simulates it
Bails seen by everyone - another player's rider falls as the rider's owner saw it: the owner's bail (and how the board was moving as it let go) is shared, each client ragdolls its own copy of that rider, and puts it away when the rider is back on the board. The bail's release velocity, position and spin now reach a rider driving from a client too, so its ragdoll flies with the board's speed instead of dropping where it stood
# Animation
Easing from a body in the world - SkateRider.BlendFromWorld( worldPose, seconds ) eases the rider's first poses from each bone's place in the world, taken into the rider model's frame where the rider is next shown, so a body whose place it takes eases across from where that body stood even if the rider moved before it was first shown
Getting up - SkateRider.GettingUp is true while the rider plays its get-up after a bail (1.6 s), so a game can wait for it to stand before handing control back
The shown pose settles as one - after every fit (the feet put on the deck or the ground, pressed, planted or lifted, the hands taking hold, the crouch, the knees' IK) the whole pose goes through the same continuity follower the clips' pose already did (PoseInertializer, each bone against its parent): whatever stage would change a bone's speed faster than the budget eases over instead, and a bone stopped dead carries on a little and settles. A carried board goes where the settled hand goes. Over a riding session (push, carves, brake, ollie, kickflip, grab, landings) the worst change of a bone's speed in a frame fell from 27.7 (a finger at an ollie's take-off) to 3.3, frames over 3 from 8 to 2 and over 6 from 2 to 0; a grab's reach from 5.3 to 1.2. Feet on the deck and on the ground, hands on the board in grabs and the board in the hand are unchanged
A standing idle that does not repeat - the shipped rider's idle standing with the board in hand is 39 s long, where it repeated every 13 s: a variation of the original generated with GenMM (patch-based motion synthesis, github.com/wyysf-98/GenMM), looping, as smooth as the original (joints' speed p99 1.14 against 1.22 rad/s), the board rebuilt in the carrying hand (held to 0.007 in). dev/genmm holds the export to BVH, the run and the way back into the pose bank, for any other clip
Animation out of the box - the package ships its rider: a clip set, the graph that plays it, the raw poses and their attributes and both hands' finger poses, under data/skater (39 MB, packed losslessly; dev/pack_poses.py). SkateRider plays them unless told otherwise (SkateRider.DefaultAnimationClips, DefaultAnimationGraph), so a game using the package animates with nothing to copy into its data folder
Pose continuity - every bone of the rider carries on from what was shown (PoseInertializer, before the board and hand fits): a bone's speed changes by no more than a budget in any frame (600 rad/s^2 turning, 150 m/s^2 moving), the rest taken up as an offset that goes back to the pose as fast as its 0.04 s half-life asks, no faster than 8 rad/s and without passing it. A clip handing over, a key's corner shown at a high frame rate, a hand pose switching in or a pose jumping in a frame shows as a quick ease: in a flip session the worst change of a bone's speed before the fits fell from 7.6 to 5.1 in a frame (at 60 fps) and the frames over 3 from 34 to 21
Grabs that hold - a hand that reaches the board in a grab or a hand plant takes hold of the shown deck where it is, closing onto it (up to 2.5 in), and stays on that place, turned with the deck, until 0.15 s before the landing whatever the clip's hand does; letting go eases back over 0.12 s. Fitted only, an indy's hand slid 6.7 in along the toe edge while it held it and a melon's never reached the deck. A grab's reach plays up to 1.6x as fast as authored so the hand is on the board before the grab is let go for the landing, which is now 0.3 s before it (was 0.4)
Catching a called board - a board called to the hand from afar flies to the carrying hand itself (its middle a hand's breadth off the palm), and that hand reaches out for it, open, as it comes (from 0.45 s out, at its speed), then closes on it as the carry takes over. It flew to where a pickup clip has its own board, by the hip, while the clip raised the other hand
Planted feet - a foot standing on the deck stays where it stands for as long as the clip keeps it there, turning as the clip turns it, and steps (lifted 1.5 in) only when the clip's place has moved more than 5 in; once the clip lifts it, it eases off its place over 0.12 s. The feet slid back along the deck into each clip's stance: 4-5 in at every ollie's wind-up, and 0.6 s after every landing a step back that read as a slide. Over a trick session the feet slid 245 in over the deck, now 90 in (mostly a push's front foot pivoting)
TwoBoneIk.TurnEnd - turns the end bone (a hand) and everything below it about its own place
Blend from a body - SkateRider.BlendFrom( modelPose, seconds ) blends the rider's first poses from a body pose (model-space bones in the rider model's order), such as the player's own body as it was, so a rider taking a player's place shows no jump
Stock walk on foot - a rider whose model has its own walk (the citizen's) takes its hips and legs from that walk while it walks or carries the board: a copy of the model, never drawn, plays the model's own walk from the rider's movement (its stride, starts, stops, turns and jumps), while the body above the hips stays the clips' and keeps the board in the hand. The copy is read whole once a frame after every update (RiderLegReader) and followed for continuity (PoseInertializer; the stock walk itself puts a foot 14 in on in a frame as it stops), and its legs ease in and out over 0.25 s stepping off and on the board, picking it up or putting it down. A model without a walk keeps the clips' legs. On foot the worst change of a bone's speed in a frame is 4.6 (at 175 fps; the clips' own walk 2.6)
# Performance
Sleeping boards - a board no one rides that has lain within half an inch and a degree of where it was for a second falls asleep (Skateboard.Asleep): its step is skipped until it is ridden, moved (picked up, knocked, placed) or reset, and it wakes carrying on from where it was put. Most boards in a busy game are lying about; asleep they cost nothing
Distance posing - another player's rider far from the camera is posed less often: every second frame from 12 m, every fourth from 24 m and every eighth from 48 m, each pose covering the time since the last. A crowd of riders costs about what a few near ones do; the player's own rider is posed every frame
# Physics
Hardest landing - Skateboard.HardestLanding sets the speed into the ground (m/s) above which a landing bails, in place of the profile's (zero keeps it): a flat landing off a 3 m drop comes down at about 7.7 m/s, off 6 m about 10.8 and off 10 m about 14. A landing tipped too far still bails at any speed

# Board
A board more than 200 in from the hand is called as the reach begins (0.34 s into the pickup) instead of when the reach clip's hand is out: it takes its flight's time to come and arrives as the arm comes out. A call from 20 m now takes about 1.3 s from the pickup's start (was about 1.7)
# Camera
On foot the camera starts behind the rider's own facing, not the board's: a board called from afar lies any way, and the camera could start in front of the rider
# Animation
Animation files are looked for among the mounted files (the package's own, or a game's assets) and then in the game's data folder, where they were only read from the data folder, and a packed file is read back to its original bytes (RiderFiles). SkateRider.AnimationClips and AnimationGraph default to the shipped rider (they were empty, the built-in animation, which an empty path still picks)
Landing hand-back - landing, the shown board goes over to the physical one from where it was shown, carried on as it was moving and slowing, over a time long enough that it turns no faster than 6 rad/s. A board turned 72-80 degrees under the feet in the air (a board adjust held through the landing) swung back in 0.15 s at 630-810 degrees a second; it now takes about 0.33 s at 340-360
Flip sweep - in the air a deck rolling over (a kickflip, a heelflip) drops away from the feet (up to 3 in, along the body's down) and the feet lift out of the cylinder it sweeps, each at no more than 80 in/s, coming back as the catch stops the roll
# Input
Riding keys a game can map - SkateRider rides on LocalSkateInput.RidingMove, and LocalSkateInput.Pad takes airborne, so a game can read its own riding keys and air buttons (the Sandbox addon maps every control to rebindable keys this way)
One trick at a time - a trick plays to its end before the next can start: on the ground, from a trick's pop to the end of its landing (the rider's OnBoard.Trick and OnBoard.Land states), SkateRider lets the trick stick go, from the keys, the mouse and a controller alike, so a pop pressed during a landing no longer cuts its animation short. In the air the stick stays free, for grabs, late flips and a flip held on. LocalSkateInput.ReadKeys takes ready: while it is false the mouse stick lets go
### Fixed
# Grinding
Slides named by the turn - a slide's name follows the way the board turned onto the rail, however near square it locks on. Centred on the rail, the leading end (the nose riding regular, the tail riding fakie) swung over the rail is a boardslide and swung away a lipslide; off-centre, the end swung toward the rail slides on it. The side of the rail the nose ended up on was measured against the deck's right on the last frame before the grind, which after a quarter turn in the air lies along the rail, so the name came from whether the board locked on a few degrees short of square or past it: a centred slide with the usual 0.22 s turn always played as a lipslide, and the same key gave a boardslide or a lipslide by how long it was held. Both of the grind's sides (the rail's and the nose's) are now taken across the deck's right as it came along the rail (GrindApproach), kept through a turn onto it and taken afresh after a half turn. On the set's rail, 40 runs with the turn held 0.12 to 0.34 s were each named the same at every turn length
Noseslides are noseslides - which side of the rail the nose lies on is now taken from the way the nose points across it rather than where its tip sits: on a noseslide the tip sits about on the rail, and an ollie into a noseslide from beside the rail played as a noseblunt every time
# Board
Late-loaded packages - a package that brings the library's code into a game already running (an entity spawned from a spawn menu) left its scene without the library's systems: the rider threw as it started (no animation library) and no rails were found. The skateboard makes the systems its scene lacks as it starts, and the components find them through SceneSystems
Shared tricks - a board's owner shares its whole state (Core/Networking/NetBoard: every SkateState part, the yes/no parts packed as bits), the controls it is given (SkateInput) and its tricks and landings (broadcast, raised again as TrickStarted and Landed on every other player's copy); its rider shares its footing and hippy jump (HippyJump.Show). Every other player's rider, which read empty controls, six state parts and its own switched-off walker, played none of the owner's tricks, grabs or flips and fell walking; its bail ragdoll, never stepped there, had no gravity
Remote tricks posed every frame - a remote rider in the air, on a rail or within 0.6 s of coming down is posed every frame whatever its distance (SkateRider's pose LOD skipped 1, 3 or 7 frames in 8 from 12, 24 and 48 m, each pose stepping the trick's clips on by the frames between: a flip from 48 m showed at 7.5 frames a second, its short clips stepped over, and it cut to the landing before it finished). Riding, walking and carrying riders keep the LOD
Shared interactions - a rider's interaction (mount, dismount, pickup, drop, throw, drop-in) runs only where the rider is owned: it moves the rider and the board. Its owner now shares which one runs and how (running out, held at the lip, just dropped in, whether the board still follows the hand), so every other copy plays its clips and lets a thrown or dropped board go when the owner does; motions played with PlayAnimation (gestures) are broadcast
Late-loaded code - Sandbox spawns an entity on the host (EntitySpawner: Cloud.Load, Clone, NetworkSpawn), and a player already in the game got it the moment before the package's code was registered there: its components were held as MissingComponent, which the engine never makes once the code is there. The hotloaded event does not come for code that is added rather than replaced; the engine does read each console variable of a package as it registers its code, so LateLoad's (skateboard_fetch_missing) runs its static constructor then, and a moment later, once, a player holding this package's components as missing asks the host to send each again, which it does with Network.Refresh( component ). Another player's SkateRider that arrives before its board can be read waits for it (found by the id its owner shares) instead of throwing in OnStart
Shared push strokes - a push stroke's start (SkateInput.NewPush) lasts a moment, which synced values all but always missed, so every other player's rider held a push without starting a stroke; the board's owner counts the strokes started (Skateboard.SetInput) and shares the count, and each copy starts a stroke where it goes up
Wheels where the model has them - BoardVisuals rests a board model's wheels and trucks at the model's own wheel and axle centres (BoardModelParts.WheelCentre, AxleCentre) instead of where the first physical pose had them, which for a board made in a hand was before the wheels dropped onto their suspension: the deck through them
Deck lean within the board's room - a board model's deck leans only as far as its own parts leave it room (BoardLayout.LeanLimit, measured by BoardPartDetector, allowing the fifth of the deck's thickness where a kingpin or bushing meets its baseplate), and a little past it, rising off its wheels by as much as that takes (BoardVisuals, up to 1.6 times the room; BoardLayout.WheelReach); its wheels never come closer to the deck than the room its lean leaves them (BoardLayout.WheelRoom). A truck reaching up against the deck (a hinge, a hanger welded to its baseplate) is bolted to it (BoardLayout.BoltedTrucks) and leans with it. A board with both trucks bolted is hinged: only its deck leans, no further than its wheels' room and never rising off them, while its trucks, their baseplates (a hinge's leaves, kept round their axles) and its wheels stay level under it, so nothing of it moves through anything else
Board destroyed under its rider - BodyConfiguration no longer throws when the rider's body has already let go of its physics (its board destroyed under it)
Wall rides that last - the wall catches the board's fall as a ride starts (over 0.1 s) and carries half its weight while it is ridden, so the board arcs down the wall instead of dropping off it; a ride ends once it slides down at 1.9 m/s (was 1). Off a kicker beside a wall, rides lasted 0.12-0.2 s, now about a second (WallRide.Carry, WallRide.Catch; physics settings are estimates, the wall ride's physics is not traced)
Wall rides that take - a board turning onto a wall for a wall ride is no longer counted as crashing into it: meeting the wall side-on as its wheels came round to it, the rider stepped off and ran it out before the wheels were on the wall (LandingSafety knows the wall being ridden, WallRide.Wall). A moment off the wall, a bounce as the board comes onto it rising, keeps the ride and pulls the board back onto the wall, where it ended on the first step off it (WallRide.LooseHold, 0.15 s). A ride starts from 2.5 m/s along the wall (was 3). Off a 24 in kicker beside a wall, turned 20 degrees into it with its lip 14 in off the wall, a board reaching it at 6.5 m/s and rolled off rides the wall every time (3 of 3 on keys and on the controller)
Board models with levels of detail - a model with LOD meshes was read as one list drawn with its first material (its vertex and index lists hold its first level's meshes alone, and every level's meshes were counted against them); its first level is now read mesh by mesh, each with its own material
# Physics
Body flips off drops - a front or back flip counted its flight only back to the height it took off from, so a drop below the take-off point gave it nothing and riding off a roof never flipped: the flight now runs to the ground seen below, as far under the deck as the ground it left was (BodyFlip.LandingHeight; BoardPilot.Apply takes the ground below, which SkateBody looks for once a step through Engine.LandingLook, the look-down the grab's let-go already used). With either ground unseen it counts to the take-off height as before
Powerslides gliding sideways - while powersliding the wheels keep only a share of their grip (so the board can swing across its travel), and nothing else slowed it: a held slide from a fast push glided sideways for over four seconds (1.7 m/s^2, on grass as on concrete). Turned across its travel the board now scrubs as sliding urethane does, up to 3 m/s^2 more as far as it is across: the same slide stops in 1.9 s (4.2 m/s^2)
Window - the map is gathered 4 m each way around the board and gathered again after 2 m (was 12 m and 4 m), contacts offered from 1 m around its bodies (was 2 m): every contact a step can take stays inside the window with 0.7 m to spare
Stalls on real maps - the board copied every collider in the scene the moment it started (or was reset) and went through every one of their triangles every physics step, and its wheel sweeps tested every triangle of the collider they hit, which on a level's world mesh is all of it: a map of 20,000 triangles and 300 props ran at 1.6 fps (627 ms frames) and a real map froze the game. The board now gathers only the collision within 12 m of itself, again as it travels, offers each step only the triangles within 2 m of its parts, and finds them through a grid over each large shape, which a board's wheel sweeps and contacts share and read once. The same map rides at 90 fps (11 ms frames, 22 ms at most); 180,000 triangles and 2,000 props, 11 ms (28 ms at most); nearly a million triangles and 5,000 props, 11 ms with one 107 ms frame as the board first reads the floor
